Market Researcher: Looking at this 2022 ad, it’s a masterclass in breaking our own rules. Visually, it’s a loud mess of diagonal stripes. Front and center is a giant yellow placeholder for a celebrity face ("FOTO TESTIMONIAL"), completely erasing our hero product from the frame. It violates almost every guideline on the sheet:
1. **Tone & Claims:** We strictly ban motivational yelling ("inarrestabile") and absolute superlatives ("più potente del mondo"). 
2. **Visuals:** The logo must be lowercase and never red. Red is capped at a 10% accent, not a dominant background pattern. Yellow isn't even in our palette.
3. **Strategy:** It relies on a famous face instead of the bottle, pushes a 35% discount (shattering our 15% ceiling), and uses a banned branded hashtag.

It sold hype, not trust. "The Only Witness" corrects this entirely by making the 600ml bottle the sole, silent focus.

Market Researcher: Looking at "Il cassetto," I see the exact opposite of the 2022 disaster. Visually, we are looking down into a kitchen drawer cluttered with old, dented, and broken bottles. Second, the lighting is real and flat, highlighting a genuine, everyday flaw in the runner's world—the graveyard of bottles that failed them. Third, the tone is *asciutto* and slightly ironic. There are no shouted superlatives or performance claims, just the quiet proof of reliability. 

It positions Rivo exactly as the playbook demands: the bottle that doesn't betray you, standing in stark contrast to the ones you threw away. This is the exact grounded, problem-first grammar we are channeling for "The Only Witness."
